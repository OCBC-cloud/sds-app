## 1. THE PROJECT IN ONE PARAGRAPH

SDSe is a mobile-first concept design tool for tensile membrane structures.
Streamlit Cloud, Python, Plotly. The user designs a structure; the app
form-finds the membrane, sizes the members, produces a Bill of Quantities,
and prepares a marketing render prompt. It is a concept tool, not a
fabrication tool. The first half of the chain. The fabricator does the
second half. Commercial goal: an engineer can use it for structural
analysis with building code checks — including CECS 158:2015 in China.

## 3. THE ARCHITECTURAL REASONING — 2026-10-03

The Chief set out the design philosophy. This is the roadmap for Stage 2
and Stage 3.

### 3.1 The reshape is a settled equilibrium, not a temporary force

When the C values (force densities) are changed in X or Y:
- Tightening in X → nodes lift.
- Tightening in Y → nodes sag.

This is the "reshape of the saddle." It is not a temporary force applied
to the shape. It is the new equilibrium the nodes settle into at the new
prestress. That is what FDM and NFDM do — they find the equilibrium shape
for a given prestress state.

Confirmed by literature: Schek's original FDM (1974), Pauletti's NFDM
(2006), Wójcik-Grąba's Extended FDM (2021–2024). All treat the reshape
as a move to a new resting equilibrium, not a temporary load.

### 3.2 Self-weight equilibrium is the resting state

After form-finding, the only load acting is self-weight (before wind,
snow, payload are added). The state that matters is the equilibrium
under self-weight alone, at whatever prestress the user has set.

Confirmed by Extended FDM: the pure form-found shape is not the resting
shape. The self-weight-corrected shape is.

### 3.3 The verdict is self-sustainability, not shape rigidity

The question is not "how much did the shape move." The question is:

"Is this final reshaped geometry self-sustaining under its own weight at
the given prestress, or does it need additional support (cables, beams)?"

**The three original conditions:**
1. No element in compression (tension-field projection does not fire).
2. No element over 50% of breaking stress.
3. Shape is the natural equilibrium of the given prestress under self-weight.

**The fourth condition, added 2026-10-03:**
4. Ponding/drainage check. After deformation, every interior node must
   have positive drainage slope. Shallow bowls trigger the ponding
   feedback loop. The Chinese standard requires this check after
   deformation, not at the initial shape.

### 3.4 The high ratio is the primary defence against ponding

The Chief's insistence on high warp/weft ratios is not aesthetic. It is
the structural mechanism by which:
- The saddle's ridge lifts.
- The valley steepens (tighter curvature, not wider).
- Water and snow drain out instead of collecting in a bowl.

A high-ratio saddle starts with drainage margin. A low-ratio saddle
starts with none.

### 3.5 Snow load must be a first-class user choice

**New recipe inputs (to be added):**

| Parameter                       | Default | Meaning                              |
|---------------------------------|---------|--------------------------------------|
| snow_load                       | False   | Whether the design must consider snow |
| critical_drainage_angle_deg     | 23.0    | Used when snow_load = False          |
| critical_drainage_angle_snow_deg| 28.0    | Used when snow_load = True           |

Both numbers are the Chief's field experience, not table values.
23° for rain (Malaysian humid tropical climate). 28° for snow (higher,
per CECS 158).

The check reports a different verdict for each climate:

- Snow-free: "Drainage slope is above/below 23°. Shape drains/ponds
  under rain."
- Snow: "Drainage slope is above/below 28°. Valley zones at [nodes]
  would accumulate snow at factor 1.1–1.5× basic snow pressure.
  Recommend higher warp/weft ratio to lift the ridge and steepen the
  valley."

## 4. WHY DRAINAGE AND SNOW LOAD MATTER — THE ENGINEERING LESSON

This is the lesson that was learned at high cost in international
practice and has been written into national codes.

**The failure mode:** snow does not fall or sit uniformly on a curved
membrane. It slides off steeper regions and accumulates in valleys.
A shallow valley collects snow, deepens under the load, collects more
snow, deepens further — a feedback loop that ends in rupture. This is
called ponding (积水积雪).

**What the codes require, and why:**

- **Drainage slope must be verified after deformation**, not at the
  initial shape. A membrane that drains when unloaded may pond when
  loaded.
- **Higher required slope in snow regions than in rain-only regions.**
- **Snow distribution factor of 1.1–1.5.** Non-uniform accumulation.
- **Safety factor 5.0 against rupture** for snow-dominated combinations
  (Class 1). **2.5** for wind-dominated (Class 2). CECS 158:2015.
- **Minimum prestress 1.0–2.0 kN/m** to prevent excessive deformation.

**Why the Chief insists on high warp/weft ratios:**

Tightening warp raises the saddle ridge and steepens the valley. Steeper
valley means water and snow run out instead of collecting. The high ratio
is the primary structural defence against ponding. It is not a design
preference — it is the mechanism by which the membrane meets the code.

**For SDS:**

The app must report, for any prestress ratio and any boundary the user
sets:
- the drainage slope at every interior node (degrees),
- any node below the critical angle → ponding risk,
- if snow_load is true: valley amplification zones,
- utilisation vs the CECS safety factor,
- verdict: self-sustaining / needs support.

That is the deliverable. Not just the shape. The verdict.
